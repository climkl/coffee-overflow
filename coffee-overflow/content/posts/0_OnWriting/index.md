# On Writing — The Start

When people ask questions like "What do you think about ___?" or "How does ___ work?", all at once a thousand images and memories flash through my mind. I desperately try to grab pieces and connect them in real time — meanwhile, they, who are still staring at me, are waiting for an answer. A colleague of mine would amusingly count the number of unfinished sentences I've attempted to say, as I stutter through seemingly disconnected words. Coherently stringing together thoughts, in a way that people can easily follow, turns out to be very difficult. 

In the same way that trying to explain something to someone makes you learn more about it yourself (or realise how much you don't know about it), I think writing is similar, just slowed down and editable. 

Writing is all around us — in our text messages, emails, forums, articles, the Bible, etc. even if we don't formally consider ourselves to be 'writers'. Hence, I want to be able to choose the right words to convey my thoughts and emotions with more clarity.

I do have some apprehensions:  

(1) I'm not a good writer  
My day job consists of me staring at code for 8 hours a day, so naturally I spend more time writing disjointed English syntax filled with symbols.

I did write essays in university. However, I exercised my freedom of choice when it came to spelling, interspersing American and British spellings however I thought matched the sound in my head best (I still do, really), which did not impress my British lecturers. 

(2) Permanency and perfectionism  
Thoughts go unheard, spoken words are fleeting, but written words linger. For an introvert, to have the inner mind spill out is actually kind of scary. As a perfectionist, what if my thoughts were premature, naive, or simply factually wrong?

Posts are not as immutable as, say, the newspaper, but there is an unspoken integrity about not editing your post from years ago, as if your 20 year old self had suddenly gained a wealth of wisdom beyond their years. Although it might be more acceptable to make an edit note if you really got something factually wrong. 

Sometimes we just have to embrace who we were, and take comfort in seeing how much we've grown since. Old art is cringey after you can make much better art.

(3) Not an expert  
But what would I write about? The internet is saturated enough — what value do I have to add to any topic that has not already been talked about? I am not a super-genius; I have no groundbreaking theorem or "the" definitive way to explain something.

Even though there are (self-proclaimed) "definitive" guides on things, you still see plenty of books, videos and articles on the same topics. Approach and perspective, style and personality, those are perhaps not just unique to the writer, but to each reader too.

My goal is to write about non-personal topics in a personal and fun way. So yes, aside from this preliminary post to convey intentionality, this is not meant to be a personal journal.

(4) Time  
Honestly, I'm not sure how much time I can set aside to write. People would say "Oh, just 15 minutes a day!" 15 minutes of writing, 15 minutes of reading, 15 minutes of exercise, 15 minutes of piano, 15 minutes of guitar, 15 minutes of drawing... you get the point, it all adds up. And I write slowly. Very slowly. In fact I've been writing this for nearly 3 hours now, and it's only my first draft.
